Thesis

02

My thesis in short

Picture a very large number N of particles moving at random, each driven by a noise (a Brownian motion), while influencing one another: each particle feels all the others through their average distribution. These are McKean–Vlasov systems, which show up in the physics of gases, in biology (collective motion), and in the analysis of some learning algorithms.

As N grows, something remarkable happens: any two particles become almost independent, and each of them follows the same limiting equation. This is propagation of chaos: if the particles start independent, they approximately remain so over time.

My thesis is about the generation of chaos: even when the particles start correlated, the dynamics eventually makes them independent. I aim to quantify this phenomenon, measuring the distance to chaos with relative entropy.

The animations on this site give a caricature of it: each dot is shaken by random noise and interacts with its neighbours. Move your mouse to disturb them.
